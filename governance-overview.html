Why This Work Was Needed

The Problem

The F&W team has accumulated a substantial body of models, code tools, and datasets over many years.

  • Structural reorganisations and staff churn have shifted ownership without it being formally transferred
  • Institutional knowledge has left with departing staff
  • Work has been duplicated because one team did not know another had already done it
  • No single person has a complete picture of what the team holds
  • Accountability has been informal and assumed rather than defined

The Response

Note

This framework is a deliberate reset.

It establishes:

  • Clear ownership for what the team already holds
  • Structures resilient to reorganisation and staff churn
  • Cross-team accountability rather than siloed arrangements

It also sets the foundation for responsible adoption of AI and machine learning. Before any of those tools can be used in operational settings, a credible governance baseline must exist. This framework is that baseline.

Roles and Accountability

Role Summary

Role Grade Core responsibility
Owner Deputy Director Ultimate accountability. Strategic decisions. Approves major changes and Gold data products.
Steward Grade 7 Day-to-day governance. Maintains standards, manages quality, oversees registers.
Delegated Steward Grade 6 Technical depth for Tier 1 assets. Code reviews, model condition assessments, annual audit.
Custodian Any grade Hands-on work. Creates, stores, and maintains assets to agreed standards.

Note

Delegation does not transfer accountability. The original role holder remains responsible.

Owner

Deputy Director grade. One per major asset domain.

Owners hold ultimate accountability for meaning, content, quality, and distribution of their assets.

Key responsibilities:

  • Approve what data and models the team uses and produces
  • Sign off on promotion of any asset to Tier 1
  • Resolve escalated disputes about data quality or access
  • Approve Gold datasets before they enter operational use
  • Ensure the team meets organisational data governance obligations
  • Consider whether team assets have value to wider government or society

Note

The CDDO model recommends senior-level ownership; Defra policy recommends SCS. This framework requires Deputy Director grade, reflecting the operational significance of F&W assets to Flood Incident Management.

Steward and Delegated Steward

Steward — Grade 7

Operational point of contact with data expertise.

  • Define and maintain data standards and naming conventions
  • Keep asset and tool registers current
  • Approve access requests and tier promotions
  • Review model quality and metadata completeness
  • Manage data lifecycle: archival, retention, disposal
  • Report risks and quality issues to the Owner

Delegated Steward — Grade 6

Named where the Steward’s strength is oversight rather than technical depth.

  • Code quality assessment and peer review
  • Model condition reviews
  • Data pipeline evaluation
  • Annual Tier 1 audit

Reports to the Steward. Does not replace the Steward’s accountability.

Custodian

Any grade. The team member who does the work.

Custodians execute within agreed systems according to rules and standards set by Stewards.

Key responsibilities:

  • Follow naming conventions, folder structures, and coding standards
  • Use version control correctly for all assets
  • Record model run settings, tool configurations, and issues encountered
  • Flag data quality problems or tool failures to their Steward promptly
  • Not share assets outside agreed channels without permission
  • Carry out disposal of assets when directed, documenting the decision, date, method, and authorisation

Note

Any team member can be a Custodian. A single person may hold Custodian responsibility for multiple assets.

The Tier System

Why Tiers?

Not all assets carry the same risk.

A script that broke at 2am during a live flood event is a different problem to one that broke during a research exercise the following week.

The tier system makes that difference explicit, and calibrates governance overhead accordingly.

  • The same three tiers apply to data, models, and code tools
  • This gives the team a shared language for risk across asset types
  • Tier describes operational criticality, not quality or maturity
  • A technically excellent piece of work begins at Tier 3 until its governance is confirmed

The Three Tiers

Tier Description Data Models Tools
Tier 1 Operational Used in live forecasting. Failure has direct operational consequences. Formally approved datasets feeding live model runs FMP, PDM, Black Box, Boundary Conditions in the forecast chain Scripts and pipelines running in live forecasting
Tier 2 Analytical Regularly used, not in the critical path. Calibration inputs, validated datasets for post-event analysis Models under active development or used for post-event analysis Visualisation tools, performance dashboards
Tier 3 Experimental Research or prototype. Not in production. Exploratory datasets, unvalidated inputs New model configurations being calibrated or tested Research notebooks, proof-of-concept tools, ML experiments

Important

An asset must not be used in live forecasting until formally promoted to Tier 1. Promotion requires Owner sign-off.

The Medallion Architecture

Two Frameworks, Two Questions

The tier system and the medallion architecture are independent.

Framework Question it answers
Tier system (1, 2, 3) How critical is this asset to the live forecasting service?
Medallion architecture (Bronze, Silver, Gold) How far has this data progressed through quality assurance?

A Silver dataset for the River Wye may be used in a Tier 2 post-event analysis today and promoted to supply a Tier 1 operational model tomorrow, without changing anything about the data itself.

The operational tier describes the use. The medallion tier describes the data’s quality state.

The Three Medallion Tiers

flowchart LR
    B["🥉 **Bronze**\nRaw\nAs received from supplier\nImmutable — never modified"]
    S["🥈 **Silver**\nQuality Controlled\nChecked, flagged, corrected\nAll decisions documented"]
    G["🥇 **Gold**\nAnalysis-Ready\nDerived products\nFormally approved for named purpose"]

    B -->|"QC process\nSteward approval"| S
    S -->|"Derivation\nOwner approval"| G

Note

Key principle: Each tier is a distinct, preserved state. Data is never overwritten in place. Moving data to the next tier creates a new dataset; the previous tier is always retained.

Bronze — Raw Ingested Data

Data exactly as received. Nothing corrected, removed, or reformatted beyond what is needed to store it.

  • Assigned a unique dataset ID: [SupplierCode]_[SiteID]_[DataType]_[YYYYMMDD]
  • Full provenance record created at ingestion: supplier, site, period, resolution, units, receipt date, file path
  • Stored read-only from the moment of ingestion
  • Even obvious errors are preserved — corrections happen at Silver, not Bronze
  • Deletion requires Owner approval and must be documented

Warning

Bronze data is an archive, not a working dataset. It must not be used directly as model input or shared externally without checking the supplier’s licence.

Silver — Quality Controlled Data

Bronze data that has been checked, flagged, corrected where appropriate, and fully documented.

The QC process has four stages:

Stage What happens
1. Automated checks Range, rate-of-change, flatline, gap, duplicate detection. Produces a flag dataset — no values changed.
2. Expert review Custodian reviews flagged data against nearby gauges and known events. Assigns quality flags.
3. Gap and error handling Each issue resolved: retain as flagged, reject, gap-fill with documented method, or reject the dataset.
4. Steward approval Steward reviews the QC record and approves promotion to Silver.

Quality flags: 1 Good · 2 Estimated · 3 Suspect · 4 Rejected · 5 No data · 6 Below detection

Gold — Analysis-Ready Products

Derived from Silver. A specific product, created for a named purpose, formally approved by the Owner.

Gold is not simply “better Silver.” It is a deliberate decision.

Examples:

  • Catchment-averaged rainfall series constructed from multiple Silver gauge records
  • Annual maximum flow series approved for flood frequency analysis
  • Daily mean flow record approved as the reference series for model calibration
  • Design event hydrograph derived from observed events
  • ML training set: named, approved collection of 15-minute Silver data with static catchment attributes attached

Important

Gold datasets require Owner approval before entering operational use. Any model or analysis using a superseded Gold version must be flagged for review.
