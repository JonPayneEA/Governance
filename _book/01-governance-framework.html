2  Data and Digital Asset Governance Framework

2.1 Purpose

This framework sets out how the Forecasting and Warning (F&W) department manages its data, code, and digital assets. It defines who is responsible for what, at every level, covering all assets from strategic accountability for major datasets down to day-to-day maintenance of individual code tools.

The framework operates at two levels. This document covers governance principles, roles, and standards that apply to all assets regardless of type. Companion documents cover detailed code-level guidance for tools written in each language. Where those documents are silent, this framework takes precedence. This framework does not replace or supersede existing technical guidance, such as the RTMDG, it is a guide to how we manage assets not construct them.

The Owner, Steward, and Custodian roles used throughout this framework are defined in the Data Ownership Model and Central Digital and Data Office (CDDO) roadmap 2022 to 2025, which is the top-level authority for data ownership roles and responsibilities across government. This framework applies those roles to the specific assets and working practices of the F&W team.

This work also directly supports the team’s obligations under Defra’s Q-FAIR data standards aims. Q-FAIR requires that data assets are of sufficient Quality to be Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable. Rather than treating Q-FAIR compliance as a separate reporting exercise, this framework and its companion documents build it in by design. Structured asset registers make data and tools findable. Consistent file formats and schemas make them interoperable and reusable. Defined access controls and storage locations make them accessible. The tier-based quality gates and quality flags directly address the Quality dimension. Compliance is therefore a natural outcome of following the framework, not additional work on top of it.

2.2 Scope

This framework applies to all data, code, and digital assets the team creates, maintains, or relies on, including:

  • Flood Modeller Pro model files and projects
  • PDM (Probability Distributed Model) configurations and parameter sets
  • Model Boundary Conditions and Connections
  • ARMA Parameters
  • Black Box configurations
  • Any configuration documentation and recommendations
  • Quality assured input datasets (rainfall, flow, stage, catchment data, boundary conditions)
  • Model outputs (flood extents, hydrographs, performance metrics)
  • R and Python tools used to support, process, run, or analyse models
  • Validation and verification records
  • Documentation and supporting reports

2.3 Governance Roles

The team uses an Owner, Steward, and Custodian model as defined in the government Data Ownership Model (CDDO, 2024). Each role has a defined grade, clear responsibilities, and can be formally delegated. These roles apply to all asset types covered by this framework, including model files, datasets, and code tools.

2.3.1 Owner (Deputy Director)

The Owner is a senior individual with dedicated accountability for a logical grouping of data or assets and in-depth knowledge of the overall business strategy in their area. Owners hold ultimate accountability for meaning, content, quality, and distribution of their assets. They make strategic decisions about how assets are used, shared, or retired. There is one Owner per major asset or tool.

Owner Grade Requirement

The CDDO Data Ownership Model recommends that Data Owners should be senior individuals but does not mandate a specific grade. The Defra Data Ownership Policy recommends SCS level. This framework requires Deputy Director grade for all Owners. This is an intentional local requirement under internal Defra group guidance and reflects the operational significance of the team’s assets to Flood Incident Management.

Owners are responsible for:

  • Approving what data and models the team uses and produces
  • Deciding who can access sensitive or nationally significant outputs
  • Signing off on major changes to modelling approach, data standards, or tool promotion to Tier 1
  • Resolving escalated disputes about data quality, access, or tool governance
  • Ensuring the team meets organisational data governance obligations
  • Understanding how assets are being used, who by, and whether they are controlled properly
  • Ensuring appropriate retention schedules are in place for all data
  • Ensuring their data assets comply with legal requirements for archival, disposal, and preservation
  • Limiting access to data (particularly personal data or data of significance to national security) to those authorised to do so
  • Ensuring security measures are in place to protect data in transit, received, or transferred to another organisation
  • Considering where data assets may have value to wider government, society, and the economy, and the protection and exploitation approaches required to realise that value (see Section 2.14)

2.3.2 Steward (Grade 7)

The Steward is responsible for day-to-day operational activities needed to support governance decisions made by Owners. Stewards are the operational points of contact with data expertise. They make sure standards are followed and assets are fit for purpose. Each asset or tool should have a named Steward.

Stewards are responsible for:

  • Defining and maintaining data standards, naming conventions, and quality requirements
  • Keeping asset registers and Tool Registers up to date
  • Reviewing model quality, version control, and metadata completeness
  • Approving access requests and code tool promotion between tiers
  • Coordinating with Custodians to ensure good practice
  • Reporting data quality issues or risks to the Owner
  • Handling data governance queries and checking with the Owner for tactical guidance where needed
  • Reviewing and updating this framework and the companion documents as part of annual updates
  • Maintaining a data catalogue or business glossary of centralised data definitions for all assets within their domain, ensuring definitions are managed and adopted consistently across the team (see Section 2.9)
  • Understanding data lineage and flow: knowing how data moves through pipelines, what transformations are applied, and the upstream and downstream impacts of any change
  • Managing the data lifecycle for assets within their domain, including archival, retention review, and coordinating disposal where retention periods have expired and Owner approval has been obtained
  • Working cross-functionally with stewards in adjacent teams or domains, sharing good practice and supporting resolution of cross-cutting data issues
  • Assisting with periodic data maturity assessments as required by the organisation
  • Periodically considering whether team data assets have value to wider government, society, or the economy, and flagging opportunities to the Owner (see Section 2.14)

2.3.3 Custodian (Any Grade)

The Custodian is responsible for capturing, storing, maintaining, and — where applicable — disposing of data and digital assets in line with the Owner’s requirements. Custodians do the hands-on work: creating, running, storing, and maintaining assets correctly. They work closely with Stewards to ensure quality by executing within agreed systems according to rules and standards. Any team member can be a Custodian.

Custodian Disposal Responsibility

The CDDO Data Ownership Model and Defra Data Ownership Policy both define the Custodian as responsible for “capturing, storing and disposing of data.” The majority of data held by the F&W team is observed environmental data (river flows, rainfall, water levels) which is generally retained indefinitely as it cannot be recreated and has enduring scientific and audit value. However, the disposal responsibility is included here to cover assets where retention limits do apply — for example, superseded configuration files, deprecated tool artefacts, or data received under licence terms that include a disposal obligation. Disposal of any data requires Owner approval and must be documented.

Custodians are responsible for:

  • Following naming conventions, folder structures, and coding standards set by Stewards
  • Using version control correctly
  • Recording model run settings, tool configurations, and any issues encountered
  • Flagging data quality problems or tool failures to their Steward promptly
  • Not sharing assets outside agreed channels without permission
  • Spotting and reporting data governance issues to the Steward
  • Carrying out disposal of data or digital assets when directed by the Owner and Steward, and documenting the disposal decision, date, method, and authorisation
  • Ensuring that changes to data content and controls can be audited

2.3.4 Role Summary

Role Definition and Responsibilities
Owner Senior individual with ultimate accountability for a data or asset domain. Sets strategic direction, approves major decisions, ensures compliance. Deputy Director grade required (see note above). May be delegated to another Deputy Director or equivalent.
Steward Operational expert responsible for day-to-day governance. Maintains standards, reviews quality, manages access, oversees the Tool Register and data catalogue, handles governance queries, tracks data lineage, manages lifecycle. Grade 7 required for Tier 1 tools; Grade 7 recommended for all other assets. For operational day-to-day management, a Technical Lead or Delegated Steward should be specified, generally filled by a G6.

2.3.5 Delegation

Any role may be formally delegated. Delegation must be:

  • Agreed between the delegating person and the delegate
  • Recorded in the relevant register with a start date and end date (or noted as ongoing)
  • Reviewed periodically or annually

Delegation does not transfer accountability. The original role holder remains responsible even when a role has been delegated.

2.3.6 Relationship to the Information Asset Owner Role

The Cabinet Office IAO guidance (updated October 2025) and the Defra Data Ownership Policy both recognise the Information Asset Owner (IAO) as a distinct role focused on the security, content, quality, and distribution of information assets. The CDDO Data Ownership Model notes that organisations may wish to combine the Data Owner and IAO roles where appropriate.

Within the F&W team, the Data Owner fulfils the IAO function for the team’s asset domain. The Deputy Director who holds the Owner role is accountable for the information assets (databases, data stores, model repositories) that contain the team’s data, as well as for the data itself. This combined accountability is appropriate given the team’s structure: the same individual needs oversight of both the logical data groupings and the systems that hold them.

Where the team’s data assets are held within wider organisational information assets (for example, data held within a shared Databricks environment or a centrally managed file store), the team’s Data Owner coordinates with the organisational IAO for that system to ensure that access controls, security measures, and risk assessments are aligned.

The Steward acts as the operational equivalent of an Information Asset Manager (IAM), carrying out the day-to-day management responsibilities on behalf of the Owner/IAO.

The following accountabilities from the IAO guidance are explicitly held by the team’s Data Owner:

  • Understanding what information is held within the team’s area of responsibility
  • Reviewing risks to the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information assets
  • Ensuring information is appropriately protected and marked
  • Inputting into the departmental information asset register
  • Ensuring compliance with the Data Protection Act 2018, the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, UK GDPR, FOI and EIR regulations, and the Public Records Act
  • Ensuring that any AI service has been assessed before it is allowed access to team information assets (see Section 2.15)

2.4 Asset Categories

The team’s assets are grouped into four categories. Each has a designated Owner and Steward.

Category Examples Owner / Steward
Model files Flood Modeller Pro projects, PDM parameter sets, boundary files Owner: Deputy Director (Modelling); Steward: Senior Modeller (G7); Custodian: District Lead (any)
Input data Rainfall records, flow data, gridded raster, catchment shapefiles Owner: Deputy Director (Data); Steward: Data Manager (G7)
Model outputs Flood extents, hydrographs, performance reports, ensemble outputs Owner: Deputy Director (Modelling); Steward: Senior Modeller (G7)

2.4.1 Asset Register Metadata Requirements

Every data asset or tool entered in the team’s registers (Tool Register, Model Register, or Hydrometric Data Register) must include metadata identifying the named individuals in each governance role. At minimum, the following must be recorded:

  • Owner: Name, grade, and role
  • Steward: Name, grade, and role
  • Custodian(s): Name(s), grade(s), and role(s)
  • IAO: Name of the IAO (or note that the Owner fulfils this function)

This aligns with the CDDO requirement that every essential shared data asset has accurate metadata including the name and roles of responsible Data Owners and Data Steward(s), and with the Defra requirement to additionally include Custodians and IAOs.

2.5 The Tier System

All team assets, whether data, models, or code tools, are classified into one of three tiers based on how critical they are to live operational forecasting. The tier determines the level of governance applied. Higher tier means more rigorous oversight, not more administration for its own sake.

The same three tiers apply across all asset types. This gives everyone a shared language for talking about risk and maturity, regardless of whether the asset in question is a dataset, a flood model, or a script.

Tier Description Data Models Tools
Tier 1 — Operational Used in live forecasting. Failure has direct operational consequences. Formally approved datasets feeding live model runs or supporting the service FMP, PDM, Black Box and Boundary Conditions used in the operational forecast chain Scripts and pipelines running in live forecasting or critical to supporting the service
Tier 2 — Analytical Regularly used but not in the critical forecasting path. Calibration inputs, validated datasets used for post-event analysis Models under active development or used for post-event analysis Visualisation tools, performance dashboards, model comparison tools
Tier 3 — Experimental Research or prototype stage. Not in production. Exploratory datasets, preliminary ingestion, unvalidated inputs New model configurations being calibrated or tested Research notebooks, proof-of-concept tools, ML experiments

Promotion to Tier 1 requires Owner sign-off for all asset types. Demotion or deprecation requires Owner approval. Assets begin at Tier 3 and are promoted as their maturity and governance are confirmed. An asset must not be used in live forecasting until it has been formally promoted to Tier 1.

2.5.1 Data Tiers and the Medallion Architecture

Hydrometric data and model calibration data must follow the Bronze, Silver, and Gold medallion architecture set out in ?sec-hydrometric-data. The medallion labels are specific to data and describe quality stages: Bronze is raw ingested data, Silver is quality-controlled, and Gold is formally approved and analysis-ready.

The medallion architecture is not optional. It exists to drive continuous improvement and force active management of data assets. Data that sits at Bronze indefinitely is uncontrolled. Data that progresses to Silver and Gold has been reviewed, quality-assessed, and formally approved for its intended use. Only Gold-tier data may be used as input to Tier 1 model runs or ML model training that may one day be promoted to operational use.

2.5.2 Model Tiers

Flood models, including FMP hydraulic models, PDM rainfall-runoff configurations, boundary conditions, and Black Box elements, are classified under the same three-tier system. A model’s tier reflects its role in the operational forecast chain, not its technical sophistication. See ?sec-flood-model-governance for full details.

2.5.3 Tool Tiers

Code tools written in R or Python follow the same tier system. A tool’s tier reflects the operational consequences of failure. Tier 1 tools require the most rigorous development, testing, and custodianship standards. See ?sec-r-governance and ?sec-python-governance for language-specific requirements.

2.6 Flood Modeller Pro — Governance Requirements

2.6.1 File and Folder Structure

Every project must use the agreed folder structure. Stewards define and publish this. Custodians must not create ad-hoc structures. The agreed top-level layout is:

  • [CatchmentID]_[Position]_[ProjectName]/
    • /inputs — all raw and processed input data (or links to position in medallion data scheme)
    • /model — Flood Modeller Pro project files (/ief, /dat, /ied, /inp, /zzs)
    • /outputs — model results and exported data (/zzn, /zzs)
    • /docs — notes, logs, reports
    • /archive — superseded versions (do not delete, archive instead)

2.6.2 Naming Conventions

Files must be named so they can be found and understood without opening them. The standard format is:

[CatchmentID]_[Position]_[ModelType]_[Version]_[Date]

Example: SEVERN_UPPER_FMP_v2.3.1_20260115

No spaces in file names. Use underscores. Dates use YYYYMMDD format.

2.6.3 Version Control

All Flood Modeller Pro project files must be managed in the team’s agreed file store or version control system:

  • Every significant change gets a new version number (Major.Minor.Patch)
  • Change notes must describe what changed and why
  • Tagged releases for any version used in a formal output or submitted externally
  • The live model and all superseded versions must both be retained

2.6.4 Metadata and Run Records

For every model run that produces a formal output, the Custodian must record: model version used, input datasets used (name, version, source, date accessed), scenario description, software version (Flood Modeller Pro build number), date and person who ran the model, and any warnings, errors, or manual interventions. Run records are stored in /docs alongside the model project.

2.7 PDM — Governance Requirements

2.7.1 Parameter Sets

PDM parameter sets are critical assets. They must be stored in the agreed repository (not on individual machines), named using the standard convention ([CatchmentID]_[Gauged/UnGauged]_[ModelType]_[Version]_[Date]), accompanied by a calibration record (period, data used, performance metrics), and reviewed and approved by the Steward before use in a formal output.

2.7.2 Calibration Records

Every calibrated parameter set must have a record covering: calibration period and events used, observed data sources (gauge IDs, data provider, quality flags), performance metrics (NSE, FAR, KGE), who carried out the calibration and when, and any known limitations or conditions where the parameters may not apply.

2.7.3 Coupled Model Configurations

Where PDM is coupled with Flood Modeller Pro, the coupling configuration (boundary locations, time-step settings, etc.) must be documented and version-controlled alongside both model files.

2.8 Code and Tool Governance

The department currently develops and maintains code tools in R and Python. These tools are governed by the same Owner, Steward, and Custodian framework as all other assets, with additional requirements that reflect the specific risks of code: unreproducible results, undocumented behaviour, and key-person dependency.

This section sets out the governance principles that apply to all code tools regardless of language. Detailed, language-specific standards are in ?sec-r-governance and ?sec-python-governance.

2.8.1 Tool Classification

All code tools are classified into one of three tiers based on their operational role. Governance requirements increase with tier level.

Tier Description Examples
Tier 1 — Operational Used in live forecasting; failure has direct operational consequences Ensemble processing scripts, alert threshold tools, Flood Modeller API orchestration, operational ML inference pipelines
Tier 2 — Analytical Regularly used but not in the critical forecasting path Visualisation tools, performance metrics dashboards, model comparison tools
Tier 3 — Experimental Research or prototype stage; not in production New model evaluation scripts, proof-of-concept tools, ML experiments, research notebooks

2.8.2 Custodianship Requirements by Tier

Role Tier 1 — Operational Tier 2 and 3 — Analytical / Experimental
Owner Deputy Director required. Must be a different individual from the Steward. Deputy Director strongly recommended. A senior manager with equivalent accountability may be assigned where agreed with the Technical Lead.
Steward Grade 7 required. Delegated Steward recommended. Grade 7 recommended. A capable team member at a lower grade may act as Steward for Tier 3 tools where the role is combined with Custodian.
Custodian Runs scheduled CI checks, monitors for issues, escalates to Steward promptly, maintains disaster recovery runbook. Runs tests before major language version upgrades, flags broken dependencies to Steward.

When a team member leaves or changes role, custodianship must be formally reassigned before their departure.

2.8.3 Routes to Tool Development

There are four routes by which a new tool enters the team’s codebase. The route must be agreed at the point of initiation and recorded in the Tool Register.

Route Description Typical Origin
A — New Standalone Tool A new script or module developed from scratch. Begins at Tier 3 and may be promoted. Proposed via a Git issue; Steward confirms route and tier within five working days. Identified gap in team capability; new forecasting requirement
B — Shared Module / Package Contribution A function or class developed for inclusion in a shared package (Flode for R, shared module for Python). Follows a promotion pathway with API review by the Steward Lead. Recurring code pattern across tools; refactoring of existing standalone tool
C — Adoption of External Package An externally developed package being formally adopted for team use. Personal Tier 3 use is acceptable; shared or production use requires evaluation and sign-off. Discovered package that meets a team need; tool shared by a partner organisation
D — Emergency / Hotfix A tool or fix developed under time pressure. Verbal authorisation from the Steward required before work begins. Forecasting failure; urgent fix required

For Route C, all external packages must be evaluated against six criteria before adoption in shared or production code: active maintenance, compatible licence, acceptable performance impact, manageable dependency footprint, genuine necessity, and sufficient community adoption.

For Route D, no more than two emergency route deployments per twelve-month period for the same tool area is acceptable before triggering a review of the underlying operational code tools.

2.8.4 Code Review Requirements

Tier Review Requirement
Tier 1 — Operational Mandatory peer review by a second team member before merging to main. Senior reviewer sign-off before first production deployment. Annual audit of all Tier 1 tools.
Tier 2 — Analytical Peer review required before merging to main. Reviewer must have at least intermediate proficiency in the relevant language.
Tier 3 — Experimental Self-review checklist minimum. Peer review strongly encouraged before sharing with others.

2.8.5 Version Control Principles

All code tools used by more than one person must be managed in Git. No shared tool may exist solely on a local machine or shared drive.

Branching: the team follows a simplified Git Flow model — main (production-ready only, no direct commits), develop (integration branch), feature/<n> (individual work), hotfix/<n> (urgent fixes to Tier 1), release/<version> (versioned releases).

Commits: all commits must follow the Conventional Commits convention <type>(<scope>): <short description> with a body explaining why, not just what. Permitted types: feat, fix, docs, refactor, test, chore.

Versioning: all tools and packages follow Semantic Versioning (MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH). Releases are tagged in Git and accompanied by a CHANGELOG.md entry.

Reproducibility: all dependencies must be pinned and committed alongside the code — renv.lock for R tools, poetry.lock for Python tools. Code must be runnable from a clean environment without undeclared dependencies.

2.8.6 Tool Register

Every code tool must be entered in the central Tool Register at the point it is shared with more than one team member. The register is version-controlled and held in the team repository.

Field Description
Tool Name Short descriptive name matching the repository directory
Tier 1, 2, or 3
Language R, Python, or both
Owner Name, grade, and role. Must be at Deputy Director level. Note if delegated and to whom.
Steward Name, grade, and role. Must be Grade 7 for Tier 1 tools. Note if delegated.
Custodian(s) Name(s), grade(s), and role(s). Any grade. Note any delegations.
IAO Name of the IAO, or note that the Owner fulfils this function.

The Tool Register must be updated whenever a custodianship role changes, a tool changes tier, or a tool is deprecated.

2.8.7 Handover and Succession

When a Steward or Custodian changes, the outgoing person must produce a handover note before they leave. The note covers: the tool’s purpose and known quirks, current technical debt or open issues, the status of any open pull requests, and scheduled maintenance tasks. It is stored in the tool’s docs/ directory.

For Tier 1 tools, the incoming Steward must run the full test suite and confirm it passes before formally accepting the handover. The Technical Lead must be notified of all Tier 1 custodianship changes.

2.9 Data Catalogue and Centralised Definitions

The Steward is responsible for maintaining a data catalogue or business glossary that records centralised definitions for the team’s key data entities, attributes, and terminology. This aligns with the CDDO Data Ownership Model requirement that agreed data definitions be maintained through Data Stewards.

The data catalogue should cover at minimum:

  • Definitions for the team’s core data types (flow, stage, rainfall, catchment descriptors) and what each means in the team context
  • The relationship between supplier terminology and team terminology (for example, mapping supplier quality codes to the team’s flag scheme)
  • Definitions for key model parameters and what they represent physically
  • Naming conventions for datasets, tools, and models, consolidated from across the governance suite

The catalogue is maintained by the Steward and reviewed as part of the annual governance audit. It may take the form of a structured document, a spreadsheet, or a dedicated section within the team’s internal documentation site.

2.10 Data Quality

Data quality is everyone’s responsibility. The following minimum standards apply to all datasets.

Standard What it means Who checks it
Accuracy Data reflects reality — values are plausible and within expected ranges Custodian (initial), Steward (sign-off)
Completeness No unexplained gaps; missing data is flagged and documented Custodian
Consistency Same datasets used consistently across models in the same study Steward
Timeliness Input data is current enough for the model’s intended purpose Steward
Traceability Every input can be traced back to its source Custodian
Reproducibility Anyone with access to the inputs and model should get the same outputs Steward or Custodian (audit)

2.11 Access and Sharing

  • All team members have read access to current model files by default
  • Write access to master model files requires Steward approval
  • Sharing data or tools outside the team (including to contractors) requires Owner approval
  • Sensitive datasets follow the organisation’s data classification policy
  • Access rights are reviewed by Stewards every six months

2.12 Data Incidents and Escalation

A data incident is any event that affects the integrity, availability, or confidentiality of team assets including accidental deletion, use of wrong input data in a published output, tool failure in a live operational forecast environment, or unauthorised sharing.

When an incident occurs:

  • The Custodian reports it to the Steward immediately
  • The Steward assesses the impact and decides whether to escalate to the Owner
  • The Owner is informed of any incident affecting a formal output or external stakeholder
  • All incidents are recorded in the governance log with a summary of what happened and what was done

For code tools: a post-incident review must be completed within ten working days for any Tier 1 tool failure or emergency deployment (Route D).

2.13 Monitoring, Reporting, and Risk Management

The CDDO Data Ownership Model requires that ownership policies include monitoring and reporting arrangements as part of the organisation’s broader risk management practices. The following cadence applies:

Quarterly: The Steward produces a governance summary covering: data quality trends from the Hydrometric Data Framework quarterly summaries, open Data Quality Issue Log entries and their age, Tool Register and Model Register currency, any incidents or near-misses in the quarter, and supplier engagement outcomes. This summary is shared with the Owner and feeds into the team’s quarterly governance review.

Annually: The Technical Lead conducts a full governance audit of all Tier 1 assets (code tools, models, and data). Findings are compiled into an annual governance report, which is presented to the Owner and submitted to the organisational risk management process. The report covers: compliance rates across all Tier 1 assets, condition rating trends for models, test coverage and documentation currency for tools, data quality trends, and any governance exceptions granted during the year.

On occurrence: Data incidents, governance exceptions, and emergency deployments (Route D) are reported to the Steward immediately and to the Owner where they affect formal outputs. Patterns of recurring incidents or exceptions are flagged in the quarterly summary and, if persistent, escalated to the organisation’s risk register.

The Owner is responsible for ensuring that governance findings are communicated to the broader organisational governance and risk management structures as appropriate.

2.14 Wider Data Value

The CDDO Data Ownership Model requires organisations to consider where data assets may have value to wider government, society, and the economy. The Steward should periodically (at least annually, as part of the governance review) consider whether any of the team’s data assets — particularly Gold-tier hydrometric data, model validation datasets, or calibration benchmarks — could have value to other Defra teams, other government departments, academic researchers, or the public.

Where an opportunity is identified, the Steward flags it to the Owner with a brief assessment of the data’s potential value, any licensing or access constraints, and any preparation work that would be needed. The Owner decides whether to pursue sharing, taking account of supplier licence terms, data sensitivity, and resource implications.

This does not create an obligation to share all data. It creates an obligation to ask the question.

2.15 AI and Machine Learning Governance

The Cabinet Office IAO guidance (updated October 2025) sets out expectations around AI governance, including that IAOs should ensure AI services are assessed before being allowed access to information assets, and that Data Protection Impact Assessments are carried out where AI processes personal data. The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 introduces additional requirements relevant to automated decision-making.

Within the F&W team, the following AI governance requirements apply:

Assessment before access: Before any AI or ML service (including commercial AI tools, internally developed models, and cloud-hosted inference services) is given access to team data assets, the Steward must carry out a brief assessment covering: what data the service will access, whether the data contains personal or sensitive information, whether the service complies with the Government’s Secure by Design principles, and whether a DPIA is required. The assessment is recorded and approved by the Owner.

ML model governance: All operational ML models (including black-box models, LSTM pipelines, and any model where outputs feed Tier 1 forecasts) are Tier 1 assets. They must have a named Custodian, a documented training dataset (as a Gold product — see ?sec-hydrometric-data), a formal condition assessment, and version-controlled model artefacts with metadata. The Python Tool Governance document (?sec-python-governance) sets out the detailed requirements for ML model development, including training/inference separation, metadata files, and ONNX archiving.

Transparency and auditability: For any ML model used in an operational forecast, the run record must identify the model version, training dataset version, and any known limitations. Where an ML model replaces a physically-based model (e.g., a black-box model replacing a PDM configuration), the replacement decision, rationale, and comparative performance evidence must be documented and approved by the Owner.

Ongoing compliance: The Steward monitors developments in AI legislation and government guidance that may affect the team’s use of ML tools, and raises any compliance concerns with the Owner. This is reviewed as part of the annual governance audit.

2.16 Review and Compliance

This framework and the companion documents are reviewed annually by the Stewards (or subject matter experts), with Owner sign-off. The review considers:

  • Whether the asset categories and roles are still fit for purpose
  • Any incidents or near-misses from the previous year
  • Changes to tools, data sources, or organisational requirements
  • Feedback from Custodians on what is working and what is not
  • Alignment with any updates to the CDDO Data Ownership Model, Defra Data Ownership Policy, IAO guidance, or relevant legislation

The Technical Lead conducts an annual audit of all Tier 1 code tools, covering header block completeness, test coverage, documentation currency, dependency lockfile consistency, and branching strategy compliance.

Spot-check audits of model files and metadata records may be carried out at any time by Stewards.
